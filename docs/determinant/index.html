Determinants & Invertibility

The dashed white square is the unit square, built from the basis vectors (1, 0) and (0, 1). The matrix A sends (1, 0) to its first column (pink) and (0, 1) to its second column (purple), so the square turns into the blue shape. Change the entries and watch what happens to its area.

The determinant is the signed area scaling factor of AAA: every area gets multiplied by it, and a negative sign means the shape is flipped (mirrored). Since the unit square starts with area 1, the absolute value ∣det⁡(A)∣|\det(A)|∣det(A)∣ is exactly the area of the transformed square.

Invertible

The transformed square has nonzero area (1), so it still covers a real 2D region. The transformation is invertible: it can be undone.
